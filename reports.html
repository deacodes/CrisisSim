1. Executive Summary

During a projected Category 3 riverine flood event (90 mm/hr peak precipitation, 2.8m river crest), standard municipal emergency operations result in 3,210 stranded residents and severe systemic inequities across low-lying districts. This policy assessment evaluates the deployment of CrisisSim’s Optimized Heuristic Strategy, which combines early auxiliary shelter activation, targeted amphibious boat dispatch, and high-water shuttle prioritizations.

The recommended strategy elevates total safe evacuations from 74.3% (9,280) to 91.0% (12,480), reduces transit delays by 40%, and lifts the municipal equity score from 0.54 to 0.82 for an incremental response expenditure of $7.8M.

5. Critical Bottlenecks & Equity Findings

Bridge 3 Structural Closure Vulnerability Bridge 3 has the lowest deck clearance in Riverton (1.8m). When flood waters surpass 0.2m over the deck at Hour 4.2, 9,800 residents in Neighborhood 5 lose direct egress. Precautionary boat deployment is essential.
Transit Dependency Compounding Delay Neighborhood 5 and Eastside Flats contain the highest concentrations of non-vehicle owners (58% and 46%). Without dedicated municipal bus corridors, these residents suffer an average evacuation delay of +3.2 hours.

6. Methodology & Limitations

CrisisSim uses a synthetic, data-calibrated city for educational decision support. It is not an official emergency forecast or dispatch system. Simulations rely on simplified 2D hydrodynamic flow equations and synthetic agent routing heuristics. Local municipal authorities must corroborate findings with physical hydrologic gauges before executing real-world dispatch orders.
