1. Synthetic-City Digital Twin Architecture

Riverton is a synthetic metropolitan area designed to exhibit realistic urban topography and demographic disparities without relying on sensitive real-world individual data. The spatial grid is constructed using a 10-meter Digital Elevation Model (DEM) featuring a central alluvial river valley, two elevated bluffs, and an engineered canal basin.

City Calibration Profile: 78,500 synthetic residents distributed across 8 discrete neighborhoods with varying income levels, vehicle ownership ratios, mobility constraints, and structural elevation.

2. Flood Propagation & Hydrodynamics

Flood propagation is approximated using simplified 2D shallow water equations with Manning’s roughness coefficient to model overland runoff and channelized river stage cresting:

Q = (1 / n) · A · R^(2/3) · S^(1/2)

Where Q is river discharge volume, n is Manning's bed roughness (0.035 for natural riverbed), A is cross-sectional flow area, R is hydraulic radius, and S is riverbed slope gradient.

3. Agent-Based Evacuation Behavior

Evacuation decision-making is modeled through bounded rationality agents. Households evaluate evacuation orders based on perceived risk, vehicle availability, mobility limitations, and family reunification buffers.

• Vehicle-Owning Households (56%): Self-evacuate via private automobile with average departure lag of 45 minutes.
• Transit-Dependent Households (32%): Must wait for municipal shuttles or walk to neighborhood muster points.
• Mobility-Impaired Residents (12%): Require direct paratransit or specialized ambulance extraction.

4. Dynamic Route Selection & Water-Depth Impedance

Agents navigate the municipal road graph using dynamic Dijkstra / A* shortest path search. Road segment impedance (travel cost) increases exponentially with rising water depth:

Cost_edge = Length · (1 + α · (Depth / Threshold)^β)

When water depth exceeds 0.30 meters on roads or 0.20 meters on bridges, impedance approaches infinity, effectively closing the corridor and forcing rerouting.

5. Heuristic Policy Optimization (Pareto Frontier)

Emergency managers must balance competing objectives. The "AI-Optimized" response uses deterministic multi-objective genetic heuristics to trace the Pareto frontier across:

Max Life Safety:
Minimize stranded resident count
Max Equity:
Minimize inter-neighborhood transit disparity
Min Budget:
Optimize asset deployment costs under municipal ceiling

6. Monte Carlo Stochastic Uncertainty Analysis

Rather than assuming static conditions, CrisisSim samples stochastic ensembles using Latin Hypercube Sampling across rainfall distribution curves, early warning notification variance, and shelter intake bottlenecks.

7. Equity Metric Math: Modified Gini Parity Index

Equity is calculated as the inverse Gini inequality ratio across vulnerability tiers:

Equity = 1 - [ (Σ |EvacRate_i - EvacRate_j|) / (2 · N · μ) ]

Where EvacRate_i is the percentage of safely evacuated residents in neighborhood i and μ is the citywide mean evacuation percentage.

8. Assumptions, Model Limitations & Ethical Non-Operational Stance

Mandatory Notice:
CrisisSim uses a synthetic, data-calibrated city for educational decision support. It is not an official emergency forecast or dispatch system.

The model assumes steady communication infrastructure, idealized vehicle speeds prior to water inundation, and static floodplain permeability. Real disaster operations must incorporate field observations and municipal dispatch oversight.
